:::manifesto
Make *everyone* a designer. Then sell their company *the guardrails.*

On May 23, 2024, Canva put a line in a launch announcement that few software companies ever get to write.

The product was Canva Enterprise, a plan for teams of more than 100 people. Partway through the post, between the brand controls and the security features, Canva told readers, "Canva is already used across many organizations – and probably yours, too." Then it offered a custom report showing how a buyer's own employees were already using it.

Most enterprise software starts with a pitch to people who have never touched the product. Canva's started with a list of people inside the buyer's company who already had.

By Canva's count, 95% of the Fortune 500 use it. More than 265 million people used it each month in 2025, and its annual recurring revenue reached $4 billion by the end of that year, co-founder and COO Cliff Obrecht told TechCrunch. Its business segment, companies with more than 25 seats, grew 100% that year, to $500 million.

Canva was built for people who say they can't design. Those people took it to work, and their companies ended up paying to manage what they'd started.

Then 2026 got hard. The cost of running AI features pushed Canva to cut its 2026 growth forecast from 30% to 20%. In early November, co-founder and CEO Melanie Perkins told staff that 1,271 people, 22% of the company, would lose their jobs, change roles or relocate. "After exploring a wide range of alternatives, I've come to the difficult conclusion that small changes won't be enough," Perkins wrote. "To set ourselves up for the next decade, we need to refound Canva."

To understand what Canva is trying to refound, start with how it got inside all those companies. Employees carried it in.

:::pull
Canva won the people who said they couldn't design, followed them into their jobs, and then sold their companies control over what those people were already doing.
:::

This deep dive follows that path. It covers the fear Canva removed, the search pages that brought people in, why they showed off what they made, how Canva spread through companies without a purchase, the enterprise product that flopped and the one that worked, the price of moving upmarket, and what AI has done to the whole machine.

A note on numbers. Canva is private. Its figures come from the company, its shareholder letters as reported by the press, its investors or outside estimates, and each one below says which.

## "I'm Not a Designer"

Canva started with a yearbook.

In 2007, Perkins and Obrecht started Fusion Books, an online tool for schools to design their own yearbooks. By the time Cameron Adams met them in 2012, it was doing "2 or $3 million in revenue," Adams recalled. It was built in Adobe Flash.

Adams had just spent "about two months traipsing up and down Sand Hill Road" trying to fund an email startup. Lars Rasmussen, a co-creator of Google Maps who was advising the founders, made the introduction. "I think we officially spent two hours in person, two separate Skype chats, and that was it," Adams said. Adams became the third co-founder and Canva's chief product officer.

Investors took more convincing. By Perkins' account, the founders were turned down by more than 100 of them.

The idea behind Canva was easy to say. Design software was built for designers. Most people who needed a flyer, a social post or a presentation weren't designers, and they knew it.

What Canva's team heard in early testing was fear. "They were like, 'I'm not a designer. I'm really scared of screwing everything up,'" Adams said of early users. "People were a bit afraid to start designing."

So the product's first job was to get people past that moment. Canva made a 23-second video of the whole product, then had new users make something themselves. "So we created this onboarding that literally got them to drag out a monkey and put a hat on the monkey," Adams said. The same exercise had them change the hat's color and search for a slice of pizza.

Perkins wanted the whole experience tuned the same way. "We continuously refined the experience until we got it to a point that people could jump in, and within five minutes had designed something," Perkins said.

The first people who stuck were social media managers. Pinterest and Instagram were new, and these managers "needed to create a lot of content," Adams said. "They couldn't afford to go and get someone to do it. And most of them were actually individuals." They were, in Adams' words, "extremely time poor."

Canva launched in August 2013 to a waiting list of about 15,000 people. About 500 people came on the first day, Adams said. By the end of the week, about 5,000 had signed up, and by the end of the month, about 20,000.

At first, Canva made money selling stock photos and graphics for $1 each, in a market where designers were used to paying hundreds of dollars to unlock one image. Revenue grew about 30% a month for two years, by Adams' account. Then Canva launched a subscription, Canva for Work, now Canva Pro, to a waiting list of 200,000 people. Within about three months it had overtaken the $1 business, Adams said.

**For a product aimed at beginners, the first thing to remove is the fear of looking bad.**

## Search for the Job

Templates took care of the fear. Search brought the people.

Canva's founders knew little about search engines when they launched. "We had very vague ideas about SEO before we launched. We eventually figured out we knew nothing about SEO," Adams said. Then they found "an amazing growth leader here in Australia who really properly set up Canva for search engine optimization."

What that growth leader built still drives Canva. "We mapped out hundreds of terms that people might search for that were related to Canva," Adams said. "We created landing pages that really spoke to people's jobs to be done when they landed on that page. We gave them an easy entry point into the editor."

The idea was to meet people at the job. Someone who needs a wedding invitation searches for a wedding invitation. Canva gave each job like that its own page, filled it with templates for that job, and put the editor one click away, with the template already open.

"We started seeing returns within three months," Adams said. "It scaled up over the next couple of years to become a massive driving engine for us. It's still a massive source of traffic."

Then Canva multiplied the pages. About three years after launch, it translated the product into eight languages, and the following year into 100, Adams said. Its top five markets today are the US, Brazil, Indonesia, India and the Philippines. Outside analysts who have crawled Canva's sitemaps count roughly 100,000 of these pages, most of them translated.

Paid marketing played a small part. "An amazing amount of organic traffic comes to Canva," Adams said. "It's only a minority that comes through marketing or other paid channels."

The same idea has now moved to chatbots. "So we drove a lot of early days in Canva through Google," Obrecht told TechCrunch in February 2026. "And so we really see ChatGPT or any of the LLMs top of the funnel acquisition platforms." Canva said people had more than 26 million conversations with its app inside ChatGPT by October 2025, and that it was one of the top 10 domains ChatGPT sent people to.

**Show up for the exact task people search for, and let the landing page be the product.**

## People Share What They Make

Search brought people in. Their own work brought the next ones.

The social media managers became "our first real evangelists," Adams said. "And then we also saw that growth loop where one passionate user on Canva sent out multiple tweets about it. From those tweets, other people got interested and brought them in, and we could really tangibly see that loop happening."

A few years in, Perkins put the scale on it. "More than 10 million people use Canva in 179 countries, and much of that growth has come through word of mouth and our users sharing about us," Perkins said.

People tend to value what they make themselves. In 2012, the researchers Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon and Dan Ariely asked people at a university to assemble a plain black IKEA storage box. The builders bid an average of 78 cents to keep their box. People handed an identical box, already assembled, bid 48 cents. In a second study, people who folded origami frogs and cranes valued their amateur work almost as highly as others valued origami made by experts, while outsiders saw the beginners' work as nearly worthless. The researchers called it the IKEA effect, and they found its limit. When people failed to finish, or built something and then took it apart, the extra value disappeared.

A template exists to get a nervous beginner to finished. Canva's users finished, liked what they made and showed it around. Every flyer on a café wall and every slide in a team meeting was a small ad for the tool that made it.

Canva also reached people before they had jobs. Canva for Education launched free for schools in December 2019. By the end of 2023, more than 60 million students and teachers used it, and 500,000 nonprofits used Canva for Nonprofits, Canva said. Its own framing was direct. "Preparing today's students for an increasingly visual workforce is more important than ever," Canva wrote.

That year, Canva said, more than 1.2 million people listed Canva as a core skill on their LinkedIn profiles.

**People share what they're proud of making, and the tool that made it travels with them.**

## Canva Comes to Work

No big company ever had to decide to use Canva. Its people did it first.

"Startups became a really vital part of Canva's growth through year three and four," Adams said. "And that's when we really started opening up Canva as a real work tool." Presentations brought in founders. Small teams paid for Pro. A Teams plan followed in 2020.

Canva's homepage moved with them. In 2013, it promised "Amazingly simple graphic design." By 2018, it read "Simply great design for you and your team," over the line "Get your team on brand." Its sign-up box asked what you needed Canva for, and one of the five answers was "Large Company (100+ employees)." In 2019, it said Canva was "loved by beginners and experts, teams and individuals."

The spread went far beyond small teams. In October 2019, Canva said more than 20 million people signed in each month and that 85% of the Fortune 500 used it. By the end of 2023, it put the share above 90%, with 135,000 teams designing in Canva at companies of more than 1,000 people, nearly double the year before. By 2024, it said 95%.

Canva hasn't said how it counts a company as a user. Our reading is that people at those companies use Canva, often on accounts they set up themselves.

Adams described what that looked like from the inside. "We'd seen huge amount of penetration of Canva into enterprises, but through individuals and small teams. They had just picked up Canva and started using it," Adams said. "So inside some companies, like 50,000 employee companies, we already had like 5,000 people using Canva. They just didn't do it in a structured way."

For a company, it's an odd position. Thousands of its people have chosen a tool, made its sales decks, job ads and social posts with it, and stored them in personal accounts. Nobody signed anything.

**The decision to use Canva was made thousands of times inside a company before anyone made it once for the company.**

## Five Thousand Users Aren't a Customer

Canva's first try at selling to those companies didn't work.

In October 2019, alongside an $85 million round at a $3.2 billion valuation, Canva launched Canva for Enterprise. Its launch deck led with the same advantage the 2024 post would. "With 85 percent of Fortune 500 companies already using Canva, the startup is launching a bespoke product catered to their unique design requirements," it said.

The product was built around one promise. "Marketing and design teams set guidelines, everyone creates on-brand designs," the deck said. Admins could hide Canva's own design library so employees saw only approved assets, lock elements inside templates, and approve designs before they went out.

Perkins described the problem it solved. "The way we think about the pain point consumers have is that people are being inconsistent with the brand, and there are huge inefficiencies within the organization, which is why people have been literally asking us to build this exact product," Perkins told TechCrunch.

Canva had data for the pitch. In a survey of 500 US marketers that it commissioned, 58% said people who aren't professional designers were creating social media graphics and other marketing materials. Only about half were confident that all their marketing materials reflected their brand guidelines.

It still didn't sell. "We basically tried to do that by putting in an enterprise package, giving some vague promises around it'll be better for large teams and brand consistency and stuff like that," Adams said later. "Got a little bit of traction, but we realized it wasn't very successful."

The problem was the shape of the adoption. Grassroots growth leaves "a very disorganized and chaotic network graph inside an organization," Adams said. Thousands of users scattered across marketing, sales, HR and every other team don't add up to one buyer.

"Finding the right decision-maker to champion it is a different play to the grassroots adoption we were used to," Adams said. "It required us to rethink what we were offering."

**Grassroots usage gives you a reason to call. You still need someone whose problem it solves.**

## Selling the Guardrails

Canva spent the next few years finding that someone.

"We did a lot of learning and properly built the enterprise product that now exists today," Adams said. Canva's own account says it spent "the last couple of years partnering closely with business leaders, IT administrators, and knowledge workers" before relaunching in May 2024.

Canva Enterprise was for teams of more than 100 people, and almost everything in it was about control. Admins could decide who saw which templates and elements in Canva's own library, and who could share designs outside the company. Brand kits held a company's logos, colors, fonts and guidelines. Templates could be locked so employees changed only what they were allowed to, and approvals caught anything before it went out. IT got single sign-on and automatic user provisioning. Larger customers got legal indemnity for designs made with Canva's AI tools, and admins could switch the AI tools off for teams that shouldn't use them.

The opener was the usage report. The launch post invited buyers to find out "how Canva is being used within your organization, and how you can consolidate that usage."

That report put a problem in front of two people. The brand team saw thousands of employees making the company's materials outside its control. IT saw another app it didn't manage. Canva had an answer for each.

For brand teams, it sold consistency. FedEx rolled Canva out to 1,400 teams in 45 countries and cut its brand review time by 77%, by Canva's account. For IT, it sold consolidation. "72% of CIOs cite app sprawl as a top concern," Canva said in 2025, citing its own survey of CIOs.

Then Canva moved the scattered accounts inside. A tool called Personal Team Archiving archives employees' personal workspaces automatically and guides them to move their work into "official, organization-managed environments," Canva said.

The sales team worked from the same signals. "Even the largest enterprise customers often begin their journey when a user signs up with Canva online," a Salesforce case study on Canva says. Canva tracks "each company's engagement from this point through to when they become an enterprise customer," and its enterprise sales team doubled in a year, according to the same case study. In 2025, Canva added resellers such as SoftBank and CDW and consultancies such as Deloitte, "combining our product-led roots with new go-to-market models," it said.

It worked. By June 2025, Canva Enterprise customers included FedEx, Salesforce, Expedia, Reddit, Docusign and Zoom. At the end of 2025, the business segment had doubled to $500 million in annual recurring revenue, Obrecht said.

That's still about one dollar in eight. Most of Canva's $4 billion comes from individuals and small teams. The business segment is the part that doubled.

## The Price of Moving Up

Canva named the shift itself at the end of 2024. "Our first decade was all about empowering every individual to design," its year-end post said. "Now, we're doubling down on empowering organizations and teams of all sizes to design and collaborate together too."

The people who had spread Canva felt the shift first. In 2024, Canva moved Canva Teams, its plan for small groups, to per-seat pricing with a minimum of three seats. For some long-time customers, the price rose "as much as 300%," SmartCompany reported, and users took to LinkedIn and Reddit threatening to cancel. Teams had launched in 2020 with an introductory rate that, by SmartCompany's understanding, gave five seats for the price of one.

Canva defended the change. "Our original pricing reflected the early stage of this product and has remained unchanged for the last four years," a spokesperson said, pointing to its investment in AI tools. Existing customers got 12 months of discounted pricing before the full price applied.

Canva reached up at the same time. In March 2024, it bought Serif, the maker of the professional design software Affinity, for a reported $380 million. In October 2025, it made Affinity's base app free.

## The Template Meets the Prompt

Then AI changed what a template was for.

Canva moved early. It launched Magic Write, an AI writing tool, in December 2022 and Magic Studio, a suite of AI tools, in 2023. By December 2023, its AI products had been used more than 4 billion times, Canva said. In 2024, it bought Leonardo.Ai, an Australian image-generation startup, to build models of its own.

In April 2026, at its Canva Create event in Los Angeles, Canva launched what it called its "most significant product evolution since launching in 2013." Canva AI 2.0 turns the editor into a conversation. "Rather than starting with a template or a blank page, you can now start with an idea, a goal, a brief, a rough sketch, even an unfinished thought," Canva said. Its own design model builds layered, editable designs from a prompt, with a company's brand applied from the start.

The template, Canva's answer to "I'm not a designer," had become optional.

Canva also became one of the most-used AI products in the world. Fortune, citing an analysis by the venture firm a16z, called it the third most used generative AI web product by monthly active users. And it made itself the design tool inside the chatbots. It runs inside ChatGPT, and in 2026 it connected to Anthropic's new Claude Design and launched an app inside Google's Gemini.

The same companies went after Canva's users. Google announced Pics, an AI design app in Workspace aimed at people without design skills, in May 2026. Figma launched Buzz in 2025, which lets designers lock templates for marketers to fill in, Canva's enterprise model built from the other side. General chatbots competed too, with image tools that "allow people to easily put together posters and presentations," TechCrunch wrote.

{{figure:rivals | Partner and rival. Canva runs inside ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini while Google, Figma and Adobe build tools for the same users. Sources: Canva; TechCrunch; Fortune; Google and Figma announcements (2025–2026).}}

Running AI for that many people cost more than Canva planned. In its update to shareholders for the June quarter, reported in August 2026, Perkins said the company had cut its 2026 revenue growth forecast from 30% to 20%. The "average cost of serving an AI task was too high," Perkins wrote, and Canva was "relying too heavily on frontier models," the expensive models it rented from AI labs.

"We decided to slow the rollout while we rebuilt the architecture, reduced unit costs and strengthened the business model," Perkins wrote. "This slowed our distribution and impacted our near-term growth." Perkins called it a "deliberate decision to get the economics right." Revenue for the quarter was still $921.9 million, up 25%.

Canva's free tier made the bill bigger. In 2025, 31 million of its 265 million monthly users paid, by Canva's count. The rest could still use its AI, and Forbes Australia described the cost of serving AI "to its mostly non-paying customers" as the reason several launches were delayed.

{{figure:payers | Canva's users and payers in 2025: 265 million monthly users, 31 million paid users and $4 billion in annual recurring revenue, of which $500 million came from companies with more than 25 seats. Company figures. Sources: Canva and Cliff Obrecht to TechCrunch (February 2026).}}

Investors marked the company down. On August 14, Blackbird and Airtree, two of Canva's earliest backers, cut their valuation of Canva by 17%, from $42 billion to $34.9 billion, Capital Brief reported. The independent valuation Canva uses for employee shares fell from $38.9 billion to $31 billion. T. Rowe Price's mark implied about $30.4 billion at the end of August, Forbes Australia reported, citing SEC filings.

Canva says the fix is working. It's moving work onto its own models, which it says are many times cheaper than comparable frontier models. "They've now managed to bring down costs of serving AI by 90%," Blackbird partner Rick Baker said in August.

**For a decade, free users were Canva's best marketing. AI turned each of them into a cost.**

## What's Left

Canva's answer arrived in early November.

"This has been our hardest year yet, and as we shared earlier this year, our growth has slowed for the first time in a decade," Perkins wrote to staff. "This is without doubt the hardest moment in our thirteen-year history." The 1,271 people affected include role changes and relocations, and Canva hasn't said how many will lose their jobs. Product, growth and platform teams are merging into one organization. Sydney becomes the main base for product teams, and most hybrid staff are expected in an office two to three days a week from January.

Perkins framed the change as a return. Canva needed to get "back to our roots of small, empowered teams with the freedom to make decisions, take ownership and focus on what matters most for our community," Perkins wrote. Canva's announcement didn't mention AI by name, ABC reported. It cited "new technology" changing how people create and use products.

Canva's two bets for what comes next both lean on the story so far.

For companies, the bet is control. Brand Intelligence, part of Canva AI 2.0, applies a company's fonts, colors and style automatically. If AI writes everyone's first draft, the company still needs that draft on-brand, and brand control is what Canva has spent seven years selling.

For everyone else, the bet is the IKEA effect. On November 1, during NBC's Sunday Night Football, Canva launched a new brand platform, "What Will You Canva Today?" It updates "What will you design today?", the line that has greeted Canva users for nearly 13 years and still tops its homepage. The campaign is built around "the personal satisfaction that comes from creating something of their own," B&T reported, and the idea that "there is still value in making something that feels distinctly personal."

The doubts are real, too.

The 95% figure is Canva's own, and Canva hasn't said what counts. The business segment is growing fastest but makes up about an eighth of revenue, Figma is selling locked, brand-safe templates of its own, and Google is building AI design into Workspace. A free tier that won 265 million people now costs money every time one of them asks for AI. Obrecht said in late 2025 that Canva would go public in the next "couple of years," and reports now point to 2027 at the earliest. On the 20VC podcast in August, the investor Jason Lemkin went further and said Canva was "probably worth [$US]12 billion" now. That's an outside opinion, well below every investor mark.

**Canva's next test is whether people still want to make things themselves when software will make them for them.**

## What Transfers

Few companies sell design software. Many sell to people who would never call themselves buyers.

**Remove the fear before you add features.** Canva's first users were afraid of looking bad. Canva's answer was a monkey in a hat and a first design within five minutes. Find the moment a beginner feels out of their depth, and design it away.

**Rank for the job, not the category.** Someone who needs a wedding invitation searches for a wedding invitation. Canva built a page for each job and dropped people straight into the work, in 100 languages.

**Get people to something finished.** The IKEA effect only works when people finish. Templates got beginners there, and finished work gets shown around.

**Count your users inside a company before you pitch it.** Canva's enterprise sale opened with a report on the buyer's own employees. If people already use your product at work, that list is your best sales deck.

**Give scattered users a buyer.** Canva's first enterprise product flopped because thousands of users had no owner. The relaunch sold control to the people who had to manage the mess, the brand team and IT.

**Know what your free users cost.** Free users built Canva. With AI, each one can run up a bill. Price and build so the base that spreads you doesn't sink you.

Perkins' memo calls for "small, empowered teams," the way Canva began. Whatever the refounding brings, the oldest part of the machine still runs. Someone who says they can't design finds a template, finishes something and shows it to a coworker.

**Any company selling to people who don't see themselves as buyers can run the same play. Get used first, count the use, then sell control to whoever has to manage it.**
